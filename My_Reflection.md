## Lifelong Learning During My Internship

Throughout the course of my internship with **mcThings**, I dedicated approximately **30 minutes to 1 hour at the end of every workday** to learning a new concept. These same **lifelong learning** habits were applied during the development of our capstone project.

I aimed to fill an entire page in my work journal with content that I wouldn’t typically learn through IoT work experience or school. Topics included:
- **High-speed digital design**
- **How thermocouples work**
- **Creepage in high-voltage electronics**

After releasing a new board revision, I’d revisit it **about a month later** and perform a **highly critical, detail-oriented analysis** of my design. This practice let me assess the board through a different lens, shaped by the knowledge I’d acquired since the release. As an engineer, this is a **humble acknowledgment** that no design is perfect—but every version can be improved.

To answer internal questions like:
- *Why is this product designed this way?*
- *How does the application influence the design?*
- *Where are my knowledge gaps?*

…I turned to a variety of **online resources**, such as:
- **YouTube channels**: *Altium Academy, Fedevel Academy, Rick Hartley*
- **Research papers**, especially in manufacturing optimization  
  - *AIM Solder* has been particularly valuable in this area

These habits have meaningfully **expanded my knowledge base**, and I plan to **carry them forward** as I re-enter the workforce.
